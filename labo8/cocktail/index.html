Origin of the word "cocktail"

There is debate about when the term was first used. According to the London Telegraph, the word is first found in print in a March 20, 1798 satirical newspaper article about what must have been a hell of a party.

Regardless, certainly by 1806, the word was being used with its current meaning. In the May 6th edition of the newspaper, the editor defined a cocktail as a stimulating liquor composed of spirits of any kind.

During the early 19th century, an animal, particularly a horse, with a docked tail, was said to have a "cock tail". By the 19th century, horses with docked tails were common.

A second theory holds that the name is derived from the term "cock tailings", the result of the practice of tavern owners combining the dregs of nearly empty barrels together into a single elixir.
